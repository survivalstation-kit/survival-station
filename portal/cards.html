Emergency cards

Short first steps. Print this page (Ctrl+P) and keep a paper copy with the drive.

First: make sure the scene is safe for you. If any phone network works, call for help — 112 and 911 work on most phones even without a SIM card. A text message (SMS) often gets through when calls don't.

CPR — adult not breathing normally

  1. Tap and shout. No response and no normal breathing (gasping doesn't count)? Start now. Send someone for help and an AED (defibrillator).
  2. Heel of one hand in the centre of the chest, other hand on top, arms straight.
  3. Push hard and fast: 5–6 cm (2 in) deep, 100–120 per minute (beat of "Stayin' Alive"). Let the chest come all the way back up.
  4. Trained? Give 30 pushes, then 2 breaths. Not trained? Just keep pushing (hands-only CPR).
  5. Use an AED as soon as it arrives — switch it on and follow its voice.
  6. Don't stop until they breathe normally, help takes over, or you physically can't continue. Swap rescuers every 2 minutes if you can.

Children: push about 5 cm (1/3 of chest depth), one or two hands. Infants: two fingers, 4 cm.

Choking — adult who can't speak, cough or breathe

  1. If they can cough strongly, let them cough.
  2. Otherwise: lean them forward, give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  3. Then up to 5 abdominal thrusts: stand behind, fist just above the belly button, other hand over it, pull sharply in and up.
  4. Repeat 5 + 5 until it clears. If they go unresponsive: lower them to the ground and start CPR.

Pregnant or large person: chest thrusts instead of abdominal. Infant: face-down along your forearm, 5 back blows, then 5 two-finger chest thrusts.

Severe bleeding

  1. Press hard and directly on the wound with a cloth, gauze, or your gloved hand. Keep pressing — don't lift to check.
  2. Blood soaks through? Add more cloth on top and press harder. For deep wounds, pack cloth into the wound and press.
  3. Arm or leg still spurting or pooling: apply a tourniquet 5–8 cm (2–3 in) above the wound, not on a joint. Tighten until bleeding stops. Write the time on it. Don't loosen it.
  4. Improvised tourniquet: a wide strip of cloth (not string) tied around the limb, a stick through the knot, twist until bleeding stops, tie the stick down.
  5. Keep them lying down and warm. Watch for shock.

Burns

  1. Stop the burning: remove from heat, smother flames ("stop, drop, roll").
  2. Cool with cool running water for 20 minutes — even if a few minutes have passed. Not ice.
  3. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells (not anything stuck to the skin).
  4. Cover loosely with cling film/plastic wrap or a clean non-fluffy cloth.
  5. Don't use butter, oil, toothpaste or creams on serious burns. Don't pop blisters.
  6. Needs medical care: larger than the person's palm, face/hands/feet/groin, chemical or electrical burns, white or charred skin, or a child.

Shock (after injury, bleeding, burns)

Signs: pale, cold, clammy skin; fast weak pulse; fast breathing; confusion; thirst.

  1. Treat the cause (stop bleeding first).
  2. Lay them flat. Raise the legs if this doesn't cause pain and there's no leg, hip or spine injury.
  3. Keep them warm — blanket under and over. Loosen tight clothing.
  4. Nothing to eat or drink (moisten lips if needed). Stay with them, reassure, and check breathing often.

Hypothermia (too cold)

Signs: shivering, slurred speech, clumsiness, confusion. Severe: shivering stops, drowsy — this is an emergency.

  1. Get out of wind, rain and off the cold ground. Remove wet clothes.
  2. Insulate: dry layers, blankets, sleeping bag, a hat. Put insulation under them too.
  3. Warm the core first — chest, neck, armpits, groin — with warm (not hot) packs or skin-to-skin contact.
  4. Warm sweet drinks only if fully alert and able to swallow.
  5. Don't rub arms and legs, don't give alcohol, handle severe cases gently. No pulse/breathing: start CPR.

Heat stroke (too hot)

Signs: very hot skin, confusion, fainting, may stop sweating. This can kill — cool them immediately.

  1. Move to shade. Remove extra clothing.
  2. Best: immerse the body up to the neck in cold water. Otherwise soak them with water and fan hard; ice or cold packs on neck, armpits and groin.
  3. Sips of water only if fully alert. Keep cooling until they are clearly better.

Heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, weakness, headache, still thinking clearly): rest in shade, drink water, cool down.

Broken bones and sprains

  1. Don't straighten a deformed limb. Support it in the position found.
  2. Splint with boards, sticks, rolled magazines or a pillow — immobilise the joint above and below the injury. Pad it.
  3. Check fingers/toes beyond the splint stay warm, pink and can feel touch. Loosen if not.
  4. Bone through the skin: cover with clean cloth, control bleeding around (not on) the bone.
  5. Suspected neck/back injury (fall, crash, diving): keep the head and neck still; move them only if they're in danger.

Make water safe

Cloudy water: let it settle, then pour through a clean cloth or coffee filter first.

BoilRolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 m / 6,500 ft). Best method.
BleachPlain, unscented household bleach (5–9% chlorine): 2 drops per litre (8 drops per gallon). Double if cloudy or very cold. Stir, wait 30 minutes. It should smell faintly of chlorine — if not, repeat and wait 15 more minutes.
SunlightClear plastic (PET) bottle, max 2 L, clear water only. Lay in full sun for 6 hours (2 days if cloudy).
Tablets / filtersFollow the package. Most filters don't remove viruses — combine with bleach if unsure.

None of these remove chemicals, fuel or salt. Don't drink floodwater that smells of fuel or chemicals.

Hidden water at home: water heater tank, toilet tank (not bowl), ice cubes, canned food liquid. People need about 3–4 litres (1 gallon) per day.

Food when the power is out

  • Keep fridge and freezer doors closed. Fridge stays cold about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours (24 if half full).
  • Throw out meat, fish, dairy, eggs and leftovers that were above 4 °C (40 °F) for over 2 hours.
  • Frozen food that still has ice crystals can be refrozen or cooked.
  • Eat perishables first, then freezer, then canned and dry food. Discard bulging, leaking or deeply dented cans.
  • When in doubt, throw it out.

Carbon monoxide — the silent killer after storms

  • Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal indoors, in a garage, or near open windows — even with doors open.
  • Generators at least 6 m (20 ft) from the house, exhaust pointing away.
  • Symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, sleepiness — often several people at once. Get everyone into fresh air now.

Earthquake

  1. Drop, Cover, Hold on: get down, under a sturdy table, hold on. No table: cover head and neck against an inside wall, away from windows.
  2. In bed: stay there, cover your head with a pillow. Outside: move away from buildings, lines and trees.
  3. After: expect aftershocks. Smell gas? Get out, don't use light switches or flames, shut off gas at the meter if you can.
  4. Wear shoes (broken glass). Check others for injuries. Near the coast and the shaking was strong or long: move to high ground — tsunami risk.

Flood

  • Don't walk, swim or drive through floodwater. 15 cm (6 in) of moving water can knock you down; 30 cm (1 ft) can float a car.
  • Go to higher ground. Trapped in a building: go to the highest floor, not a closed attic (you could be trapped) — onto the roof only if necessary, and signal for help.
  • Floodwater carries sewage and chemicals: wash hands, clean cuts, throw out food that touched it.
  • Don't touch electrical equipment if you're wet or standing in water.

Fire at home

  • Get out, stay out. Crawl low under smoke. Feel doors with the back of your hand — hot means use another way.
  • Clothes on fire: stop, drop, roll. Pan fire: lid on, heat off, never water.
  • Wildfire nearby: leave early. Trapped: go to a cleared area or body of water, lie face-down, cover with non-synthetic clothing.

Signal for rescue

  • Three of anything means distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 fires in a triangle, 3 flashes.
  • SOS: · · · — — — · · · (3 short, 3 long, 3 short) by light, whistle or tapping.
  • Ground signs for aircraft (large, high contrast): V = need help, X = need medical help, → = we went this way.
  • A mirror or phone screen flashed at a plane or rescuers can be seen for many kilometres.
  • Save phone battery: airplane mode, lowest brightness; turn on only to check for signal at set times.

Calling for help: say "MAYDAY" (life-threatening) or "PAN-PAN" (urgent), three times, then who you are, where you are, what's wrong, how many people. Priorities — the rule of 3

You can survive roughly 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in harsh weather, 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food. Work in that order: safety and breathing, then warmth/shelter, then water, then food.
